# Flock camera and cardboard-sign research

## Executive finding

The broader backlash against Flock Safety and automated license-plate readers is real, organized, and growing.[3][4]

Verified activity includes camera mapping, contract challenges, policy proposals, local-government pressure, and some vandalism.[3][4]

Our source review did **not** find reliable reporting that supports the narrower claim of a large, coordinated movement in which people stand in front of Flock cameras with cardboard signs to stop recording.[unverified]

That image works as a campaign concept, but the site should not present it as a documented nationwide tactic.[unverified]

## What the cameras actually do

Flock describes its fixed license-plate readers as solar-powered and LTE-connected devices that generate searchable vehicle records, real-time alerts, and vehicle-description data such as make, model, color, location, and time.[1]

EFF describes ALPRs more broadly as systems that automatically capture plates, location, date, time, vehicle photos, and sometimes the surrounding scene. EFF argues that aggregated records can reveal travel patterns and visits to sensitive locations.[2]

## What the verified movement looks like

AP reported on August 13, 2026 that Flock operates a nationwide network searchable across jurisdictions and that public opposition has grown around data sharing, immigration enforcement, unauthorized personal searches, and warrantless mass-surveillance concerns.[3]

AP also reported that more than 50 agencies or jurisdictions had canceled, suspended, rejected, or deactivated Flock contracts or cameras since the beginning of 2026, based on a tracker maintained by DeFlock. The same report says cameras have been vandalized, but it does not describe an organized cardboard-sign campaign.[3]

DeFlock identifies itself as an open-source project mapping license-plate readers across the United States. Its map and associated organizing are a concrete example of the real movement's emphasis on visibility, documentation, and local oversight.[4]

## Copy direction for the site

| Direction | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Cardboard | Treat the sign as a visual symbol for making invisible surveillance visible. |
| Claims | Do not claim that one sign disables a network, deletes records, or represents thousands of coordinated participants. |
| Facts | Ground the page in vehicle data, time/location records, sharing, audit controls, retention, and local-government contracts. |
| Movement | Describe mapping, public records, council pressure, policy fights, and contract cancellations. |
| Disclosure | Keep the concept's fictional disclosure in one explicit banner immediately above the footer. |
| Safety | State clearly: no trespassing, roadway obstruction, tampering, damage, or interference with equipment. |
